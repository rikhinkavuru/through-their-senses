# Validation: does the proxy listener match real listeners with hearing loss?

Generated by `write_results_md.py` from `results.json` (produced by `analyze_v2.py`).

## Setup

- **Data:** Clarity Prediction Challenge 2 (CPC2) evaluation split, tracks 1–3 pooled: 897 hearing-aid-processed sentences in noise, 15 listeners with hearing loss, each with an audiogram and the share of words they repeated correctly (CC BY-SA 4.0).
- **Proxy listener (production code, `hearing/app`):** Cambridge MSBG hearing loss simulation with the listener's audiogram per ear → Whisper; words with probability < 0.4 count as unclear; prediction = better ear (max over ears) share of prompt words correct. Whether to add an internal noise floor at the ISO 226 threshold before Whisper was decided on the training split (below).
- **Levels:** in Clarity data 0 dBFS corresponds to 100 dB SPL (https://claritychallenge.org/docs/cec2/data/cec2_data); our MSBG wrapper treats 0 dBFS as 120 dB SPL, so inputs are offset by −20 dB to present the same sound pressure.
- **Calibration:** 5-fold GroupKFold by listener; logistic 100/(1+exp(-k(x-x0))) fit per training fold. Only two numbers are fitted per fold; nothing is trained on CPC2 otherwise.
- **Uncertainty:** 2000 resamples of signals, seed 0, percentile 95% CI.
- **Noise floor:** internal noise floor ON (v2, exact ISO 226 shaping); chosen on the CPC2 training split by the pre-registered rule (SELECTION.md).

## Model selection (training split)

Before looking further at the test set, the noise-floor setting was chosen by a rule written down in advance (`SELECTION.md`, committed before the run): Whisper base.en on a seeded subset of 600 CPC2 training sentences, floor on vs off, lower listener-grouped-CV RMSE wins, ties within 0.5 points keep the floor.

| Training candidate | Calibrated RMSE | Pearson r |
|---|---|---|
| A_floor_on_v2 | 25.09 | 0.761 |
| B_floor_off | 24.80 | 0.767 |

**Chosen:** A_floor_on_v2. The other setting is reported on the test set under Ablations.

Stated plainly: on the test set the setting that was not chosen scores better (small.en: RMSE 24.7, r 0.78), (base.en: RMSE 25.1, r 0.78). It was not adopted because the decision was made on training data, where the two were within the pre-registered tie margin; switching now would be choosing on test labels.

Lower RMSE (percentage points of words correct) and higher Pearson correlation are better. Brackets are 95% bootstrap intervals.

## Word level

On 423 sentences where the listener got some but not all words right, we compared the words the proxy missed with the words the real listener missed (from their transcribed responses). Precision 0.60, recall 0.78, F1 0.68. If the proxy placed its misses at random within each sentence, precision would be 0.52.

## Correction made during this study

A unit test (`hearing/tests/test_sim.py`) found that the first version of the internal noise floor, an FIR filter design, sat 7–9 dB above the ISO 226 threshold between 250 and 4000 Hz because the filter could not follow a target spanning more than 40 dB. It was replaced with exact frequency-domain shaping (band levels now within about 1.5 dB of ISO 226) and the production configurations were decoded again. The earlier results are kept above as “floor_v1_superseded”. The corrected floor then scored worse than no floor on the test set, which is why the setting was re-decided on the training split instead of being picked from test results.

## Limits

- CPC2 listeners heard hearing-aid-processed signals over headphones; the app simulates free-field speech. The validation checks the listener model, not the whole app.
- 15 listeners is a small panel; confidence intervals resample sentences, not listeners.
- The logistic calibration is fitted on test listeners (grouped CV, so never on the listener being predicted). Published systems used the separate training set.
- The deployed app runs Whisper base.en on different hardware (x86 int8 kernels); transcripts can differ slightly from these runs.
